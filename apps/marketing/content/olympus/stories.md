# Evidence before applause.
A strong story does not begin with a dramatic result. It begins with a clear
relationship question and shows how the evidence moved from connection to
perspective to outcome.
## What every published story should show
1. **The relationship question:** What did the team need to understand or
   decide?
2. **The connection paths:** Which applications, systems, campaigns,
   communications, APIs, imports, webhooks, or agents held the evidence?
3. **The evidence boundary:** What was observed, resolved, inferred, missing,
   stale, or still under review?
4. **The perspective:** What relationship, journey, episode, or decision became
   visible?
5. **The action:** What was reviewed, approved, delivered, or changed?
6. **The outcome:** What was actually observed afterward?
## Story states
- **Synthetic demonstration:** seeded data used to explain the product flow;
  not a customer result or benchmark.
- **Pilot:** a bounded deployment with a named scope, baseline, and review
  window.
- **Production result:** a measured result tied to a real deployment context.
- **Approved publication:** evidence reviewed by the appropriate customer,
  product, legal, and marketing owners.
We do not invent customer stories. Until an approved customer result exists,
the public site should use the synthetic loop and invite proof partners to help
document a governed pilot.

# A perspective should show where it came from.
Aether's trust model starts with boundaries around the connections and ends with
an explainable perspective.
## Core trust controls
### Tenant-scoped boundaries
Identity, relationship, and operational evidence remain within the authorized
tenant scope. Cross-tenant enrichment is not an implied feature.
### Consent-aware collection
Consent and processing context can determine whether evidence may be collected,
linked, projected into the graph, retained, or used for a downstream action.
### Provenance and freshness
Perspectives retain the source, timestamp, ingestion context, and freshness
needed to understand what the system actually saw.
### Visible confidence and quality
Resolved and inferred relationships retain their confidence and quality context.
Missing, stale, denied, and unavailable states do not silently become certainty.
### Governed action
Recommendations and deliveries can require explicit human approval. The system
can support the decision without becoming the authority by default.
### Audit and export
Governed activity should be reviewable through the deployment's audit and export
paths, subject to the enabled product surface and retention policy.
## Availability is part of trust
Service availability is separate from provider and feature readiness. A healthy
service does not mean every connector, lens, SDK fleet, or agent capability is
live for every tenant.
Public readiness states are Available, Gated, Pilot, Unavailable, and Planned.
The current deployment—not a registry entry or a page of documentation—decides
which state applies.
## What Aether does not claim by default
Aether does not claim universal provider availability, formal certification,
classified-workload readiness, guaranteed financial outcomes, custody, trading,
campaign buying, data residency, or irreversible autonomous execution without
specific current evidence and deployment conditions.
